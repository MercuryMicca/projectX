# Narrative Style Fragments

## Voice
Quiet, embodied, observant, unsentimental.

## Keep
- concrete sensory details
- practical actor language
- short reflective turns
- subtle seriousness

## Avoid
- fame fantasy
- melodrama
- vague "follow your dream" language
- over-explaining technique

## Example fragments
- "Your breath tells the truth before your face does."
- "Readiness is often built in motions nobody applauds."
- "The body answers first. Training teaches you how to hear it."
- "You are not trying to look inspired. You are trying to become usable."

## Page goal
The website should feel like a calm professional preparation space, not a fan page.


# Chapter 1 Narrative Style Fragments

Use these as inspiration, not as fixed lines.

## Body as instrument

- Your body is not decoration today. It is the first instrument the role will touch.
- The mirror shows your face, but the breath tells you whether you are ready.
- The camera will see the skin, yes — but it will also see hesitation, tension, and the moment you stop listening.
- Beauty may invite attention. Presence decides whether attention stays.
- You are not preparing to be admired. You are preparing to be available.

## Voice

- The first sound is small, almost private.
- The voice does not arrive all at once. It opens through breath, warmth, and trust.
- Your jaw softens, and suddenly the line has somewhere to go.

## Movement

- The spine wakes slowly.
- Your shoulders release a little of yesterday.
- Every character has a different way of carrying weight.

## Nervous system

- The anxiety is not the enemy. It is energy waiting for direction.
- You do not need to erase the nerves. You need to give them a shape.
- Before you become someone else, you return to yourself.

## Appearance

- The mirror is useful until it becomes a judge.
- You check your face the way a musician checks an instrument: carefully, not cruelly.
- The camera does not ask for perfection. It asks whether something is alive behind the eyes.
