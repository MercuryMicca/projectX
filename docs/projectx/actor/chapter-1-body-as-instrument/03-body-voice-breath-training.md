# Body, Voice, Breath Training

## Training principle
Actors do not only "feel more." They condition breath, alignment, articulation, and responsiveness so feeling can travel clearly.

## Training ideas
- breath drops low before speech becomes usable
- jaw, tongue, neck, and ribs affect vocal freedom
- posture changes emotional availability
- physical tension often masks as personality

## Practices that may appear in Chapter 1
- slow nasal breathing
- humming and resonance checks
- articulation warmups
- spine roll-downs
- shoulder release
- grounding through the feet

## Narrative use
When the user makes choices, the story can connect those choices to readiness:
- rushing narrows breath
- breath widens attention
- physical care changes presence


# Body, Voice, and Breath Training

The actor's body is often treated as an instrument. Training may include voice, movement, breath, posture, rhythm, tension release, and physical awareness.

Important concepts:
- Breath supports voice and emotional regulation.
- Jaw, tongue, neck, and shoulder tension can affect vocal freedom.
- Posture affects presence and character.
- Movement reveals personality, status, age, confidence, fear, and hidden tension.
- Eyes and stillness are part of performance, especially on camera.
- Repetition builds reliability.
- Physical actions can help create emotional truth.

Voice training may include:
- Breath warm-up
- Humming
- Resonance
- Articulation
- Projection
- Vocal range

Movement training may include:
- Stretching
- Spine mobility
- Walking in character
- Gesture awareness
- Center of gravity
- Tempo and rhythm
- Stillness

The actor does not train the body only to look good. The actor trains the body so emotion can move through it clearly.
